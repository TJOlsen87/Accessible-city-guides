Accessible City Guides by TJ Olsen

Restaurant, bar, coffee, nightlife, and neighborhood guides built with accessibility, practical navigation, and real-world usefulness in mind.

About the project

These guides grew out of my work in hospitality, live music, events, accessibility, and the very ordinary problem of wanting to know where to eat, drink, meet people, or grab coffee without fighting through inaccessible menus and vague recommendation lists.

The goal is not to catalog every business in a city. Instead, each guide is curated around places that are useful, distinctive, affordable when possible, culturally interesting, unusually good, or simply worth knowing about.

Whenever possible, I separate firsthand experience from research. I also include practical information such as price, drinks, meal type, neighborhood, distance, accessibility notes, and situational cautions. A place does not need to serve alcohol to be included, and a high rating elsewhere does not automatically make it a recommendation here.

This is a living project. Restaurants close, menus change, ownership changes, and neighborhoods keep moving. Entries will be updated as I revisit places, hear from locals, and build new guides.

Accessibility approach

The site itself is intentionally simple: semantic headings, ordinary links, keyboard-friendly controls, readable text, and minimal visual clutter. Where restaurant menus or websites are inaccessible, I try to provide alternate information rather than treating accessibility as an afterthought.
